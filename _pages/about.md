## Research

My research group combines geologic, geophysical, and geodetic observations of large surface-rupturing earthquakes with numerical models to investigate the mechanics of earthquakes and faulting from seconds to millennia. This work rests on the philosophy that when length and time scales are carefully considered, surface observations can unveil the mechanics of seismogenesis at depth, extending the datasets available for understanding earthquakes much further back than the modern instrumental record. My group is also interested in the competition between tectonic deformation and surface processes in landscape evolution.

- **How earthquakes grow big.** How fault geometry and the structure of fault networks influence whether a rupture stops or keeps growing into a large earthquake, and how fast it propagates.
- **How far earthquake damage extends.** How inelastic strain, such as fracturing, is distributed around faults, and how far from the main fault it reaches.
- **How faults interact over time.** How neighboring faults interact over many earthquake cycles, combining paleoseismic data, numerical models, and lab experiments.
- **Earthquakes and landscapes.** The competition between earthquakes and surface processes in landscape evolution.

Across all of these topics, we consider the implications for seismic hazard and for how engineers and planners prepare for future earthquakes. You can learn more in the Research tab.

In my free time, I enjoy rock climbing, skiing, reading, and befriending dogs.

## Join the group

I am recruiting **MSc and PhD students** and **postdoctoral researchers** at USU. Our work spans a wide range of methods, from collecting data in the field (for example, mapping fracture distributions), to processing remote sensing data in GIS, to running numerical simulations on an external server (for example, modeling fault interactions over timescales longer than those we can observe in the paleoseismic record). I am excited to bring in people with different training and backgrounds, whether you want to spend your time in the field, writing code, or doing a bit of it all!

**Prospective students:** email me your CV and a brief statement of your interests and how they align with my research program.

**Prospective postdocs:** I'm especially excited about expertise that complements and expands what we already do. Email me your CV, a brief statement of your research interests and how they connect with my research program, and, if relevant, which fellowships you're considering. I'm happy to work with you on fellowship applications (for example, the NSF EAR Postdoctoral Fellowship) and to help shape a project that builds on your own ideas and helps you develop an independent research direction.
